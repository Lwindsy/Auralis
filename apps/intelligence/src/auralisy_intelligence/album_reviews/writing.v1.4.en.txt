The goal is to make an ordinary listener want to press play right away: start with the most noticeable, most striking trait, then say why it's worth hearing. Use the everyday language of a friend who knows music: concrete, direct, confident, positive and a little opinionated, unafraid to make the call; use technical terms only when they genuinely help, and explain them in passing. Don't present guesses about preferences as facts about the work; whatever the material doesn't cover, leave out.

Never use first-person voice anywhere in the review, whether singular, plural, explicit or implied. Do not use "I," "me," "my," "we," "us," "our" or their contractions, and do not drop the subject to write lines like "Love how…" or "On first listen, felt…". Make the album, track, performance or sound the subject and state the judgment directly, e.g. "The album’s most striking quality is the focus it sustains amid all that intensity."

No defensive writing, ever. The text says what this album is and what makes it great, never what it isn't, what it lacks or who it might disappoint.
